# Prompt for Fable 5 — Three Strategic Options for Maru Online

> Copy everything below the line into a fresh Fable 5 conversation. It is self-contained.

---

You are acting as a strategy advisor to the founder of a two-year-old South African AI integration consultancy. I need you to propose **three genuinely different options** for what this business should do over the next 90–180 days, then recommend one.

Read the whole brief before answering. Be critical rather than agreeable — if you think the question I'm asking is the wrong one, say so explicitly and explain why before you answer it.

## 1. The business

**Maru Online** (Pty) Ltd — Gauteng, South Africa. Formerly Aurellius Group. Solo founder-operator (Jimmy Motsei), no employees. Serves South African SMEs.

**Positioning** (formally documented, last revised 9 May 2026):

> "Maru is the integration consultancy that connects what businesses already have — tools, processes, and people — to deliver AI workflows that improve operating efficiency without changing headcount."

The positioning is built on three stacked claims:
- **Strategic:** integration, not invention. Maru connects existing systems rather than building novel AI.
- **Acquisition:** diagnosis-led, not solution-led. A free assessment is the wedge; a paid diagnostic qualifies.
- **Retention:** measured, not delivered. Engagements end at proof — hours redirected, rands of capacity unlocked.

Deliberately ceded territory: novel AI product building, enterprise transformation, AI-generated marketing content, and all tool-vendor referral margin (Maru is platform-agnostic and takes no commission on HubSpot/Make/Zapier).

**Service model:** four phases — Diagnose (48h) → Design (5–7 days) → Build (20–30 days) → Launch & Measure (30 days support). Fixed pricing from R18,000.

**Market context:** South Africa, ~32% unemployment. The "without changing headcount" clause is commercially differentiating locally, not merely ethical. WhatsApp is the dominant SME customer channel. Buyers are price-sensitive and mobile-first. The stated market barrier is an AI *skills shortage*, not lack of AI awareness.

## 2. Current commercial reality — be blunt with this

- The website was rebuilt and shipped **4 May 2026**. It is roughly three months old.
- **Revenue is pre-traction.** The first-ever client invoice (INV-000001, R55,000, Simelane Refrigeration) was **cancelled — the client could not pay**. The build work was salvaged and is being repurposed into a Maru-owned WhatsApp SaaS product.
- **One live beachhead opportunity:** "Growth Firm / GrowthIQ" — a B2B2B play where corporates pay Growth Firm to run supplier-development programmes for their SME portfolios. Maru is building their digital infrastructure and AI layer. Phase 1 approved 2 July 2026. Their revenue milestone is R1m+ in the first fiscal year. This is Maru's engagement **one of five** — sector-specific positioning is deliberately locked until five documented engagements exist.
- **Advisory board in formation:** propositions sent 27 April 2026 to Shepherd Shonhiwa (IoDSA Chair; board experience across media, energy, financial services, manufacturing) and Benedict Ditshego (ex-Dark Fibre Africa executive). Neither has formally confirmed. No equity offered at this stage.
- **Compliance overhang:** the company is CIPC annual-return non-compliant (deregistration risk), and received a PAIA compliance notice from the Information Regulator on 22 May 2026.
- **Zero published case studies.** No client engagement has ever had before/after numbers published.
- **Capacity:** roughly 5–8 focused hours per week available for anything that isn't client delivery.

## 4. Your task

Propose **three distinct strategic options** for the next 90–180 days.

**They must be genuinely different bets, not three intensities of the same plan.** Specifically, each option must differ in *where the next ten clients come from* — not merely in tactics or budget. If two of your options share the same primary acquisition channel, you have not given me three options.

For each option, give me:

1. **The bet in one sentence** — what you are wagering the business on.
2. **The reasoning** — why this is credible given the evidence above, not generic best practice.
3. **What it requires** — time, money in rands, skills, tools. Assume 5–8 hours a week and minimal cash.
4. **First 30 days** — concrete, specific actions, not themes.
5. **Leading indicator** — the single metric that tells you within 30 days whether it's working. Do not use vanity metrics.
6. **Kill criteria** — what would have to be true for you to abandon this and switch.
7. **Honest risks** — including the ones that make the option unattractive.
8. **What it forecloses** — the opportunity cost.

Then:

- **Recommend one**, and defend the choice against the other two directly.
- **Say what you would do about the website specifically** under your recommendation — it may be very little, and that is an acceptable answer.
- **Name your key assumptions** and what evidence would overturn them.

## 5. Constraints and standards

- **South African market realities are binding.** Do not propose tactics that assume US/European conditions — cheap paid acquisition, large addressable inbound volume, high card-on-file SaaS conversion, or buyers who fill in web forms. Consider load-shedding, data costs, WhatsApp primacy, POPIA, B-BBEE, SETA/skills-levy funding, and SA payment behaviour where relevant.
- **Cash is the binding constraint.** The first invoice was never paid. Prefer options that generate revenue before requiring it. Any option needing meaningful upfront spend must justify it explicitly.
- **Solo-operator capacity is the second constraint.** An option that requires hiring, or more than ~8 hours a week of non-delivery work, must say so plainly.
- **Do not propose "produce more content" as a standalone option** unless you can defend it against the 42-organic-sessions-in-two-years evidence.
- **Do not pad.** No filler, no restating my brief back to me, no generic marketing-textbook advice. Every claim should be traceable to something in this brief or to a clearly-labelled assumption.
- Use South African English and quote all figures in rands.

## 6. A caution

I have deliberately framed this as "fix and improve the analytics performance." **I suspect that framing is wrong** — the data may be telling me the constraint sits somewhere other than the website entirely. Interrogate that. If the right answer is that web performance is a symptom and not the problem, say so up front, then give me three options against the *real* constraint instead.

Begin with your assessment of whether I'm asking the right question. Then give me the three options.

# Maru Online — Copywriting Rules
**Reference for all website copy. Read before writing or editing any text.**

---

## Core Philosophy

If you have to choose between clear and creative, choose clear.
If you have to choose between honest and clever, choose honest.

The job of copy is not to impress — it is to be believed.

---

## The Trust Stack

Copy must work through four layers in order. If you skip to action before earning recognition and credibility, it will not convert.

1. **Recognition** — "This person understands my world"
2. **Credibility** — "They've done this before"
3. **Relevance** — "This is actually for a business like mine"
4. **Action** — "I know what to do next"

---

## Language Rules

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| "utilize" | "use" |
| "facilitate" | "help" |
| "streamline your operations" | name the actual problem |
| "innovative solution" | describe what it actually does |
| "world-class" / "best-in-class" | a specific proof point |
| "optimize" | what specifically gets better |
| "leverage" | "use" |
| "end-to-end" | "start to finish" — or name what's included |
| "operationalize" | say what you actually mean |
| "revenue infrastructure" | don't — rejected framing |
| "revenue systems" | don't — rejected framing |
| "digital transformation" | means nothing to the reader — describe the change |
| "holistic approach" | never |
| "strategic partner" | not until the relationship exists |
| "cutting-edge" / "state-of-the-art" | never |

**Simple over complex.** Grade 9 reading level. Most readers are on mobile, between meetings, often not reading in their first language.

**Active over passive.** "We build your system" not "Your system will be built."

**Confident over qualified.** Remove "almost," "very," "really," "quite," "sort of."

**Specific over vague.** "We cut their admin time by 6 hours a week" beats "We save you time." If you don't have the number, describe the situation: "No more chasing invoices on a Friday afternoon."

---

## Feature/Benefit Translation

Always complete the translation:

- **Feature:** What it does
- **Benefit:** What that means for the customer
- **Context:** Why it matters right now

Example:
❌ "AI-powered document processing"
✅ "Your quotes go out the same day — not three days later when the client's already called someone else."

## Page Structure Rules

### Hero Section
1. **Headline** — Name the outcome or the problem. Not the company.
2. **Subheadline** — Who it's for + how you do it
3. **CTA** — One action. Specific. Not "Learn More."
4. **Trust signal** — One line. Years of experience, sector experience, or founder credibility.

Headline test: Can a stranger read it in 3 seconds and know exactly what this business does? If not, rewrite.

### Services / What We Do
Each service section must answer:
1. Who is this for?
2. What exactly do you get?
3. What does life look like after?

### About Page
- Founder's experience stated plainly (not modestly, not arrogantly)
- Why they do this work (authentic, not corporate mission speak)
- Who they've worked with — sector, size, type
- A human signal — photo, real location, real person

Cut: Mission/vision/values boilerplate. "Passionate about" anything. Generic "team of experts."

### Calls to Action

| Buyer Stage | Weak CTA | Strong CTA |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | "Contact Us" | "See how it works — book a 20-min call" |
| Consideration | "Get Started" | "Get a free assessment of what's slowing you down" |
| Decision | "Submit" | "Let's talk — no pitch, just a conversation" |

Lower-commitment CTAs convert better initially. "Book a call" outperforms "Get a quote" until trust is established.

---

## Trust Signals (Not Optional)

- **Specificity of experience:** Name the sectors, the types of businesses
- **Proof of work:** Before/after descriptions (even anonymous)
- **Availability signals:** Response time, WhatsApp contact, local number
- **Real humans:** Named people, real photos, LinkedIn links
- **Risk reduction:** What happens if it doesn't work? Mention it before they think of it.

---

## What to Never Do

- Lead with company history before the client's problem
- Use "South African" in positioning statements (lives in content layer only)
- Fabricate testimonials, client names, or statistics
- Use unattributed numbers ("187% increase") without named source
- Use corporate paragraph structure — bottom line up front, always
- Be too formal (creates distance) or too casual (erodes B2B credibility)
- Say "We're so excited to..." — no one believes this

---

## After Drafting

1. Read it aloud. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
2. Remove every sentence that talks about you before it talks about the client.
3. Specificity check: replace every vague claim with a number, name, or concrete situation.
4. Verify the CTA is the only action on the page.
